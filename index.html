How it works

No phone-number discovery. No strangers.

MEYA has no public directory and doesn't search your address book. Every connection starts with two people in the same room.

  1. Meet and scan

    One person shows their MEYA QR code. The other scans it. The code carries their identity key, so nobody in between can swap it.

  2. Accept the request

    The person you scanned accepts. MEYA checks that the reply matches the exact key in the QR code before it trusts the contact.

  3. Talk privately

    Messages are encrypted on your phone and decrypted only on theirs. If a contact's key ever changes, MEYA warns you instead of switching silently.

Encryption

Three ratchets, braided together

MEYA is being built on the Triple Ratchet, the protocol Signal introduced in 2025. A classical ratchet and a post-quantum ratchet each produce a key for every message, and the two keys are mixed with HKDF. An attacker would have to break both.

Sessions begin with PQXDH, which combines X25519 with ML-KEM-1024. After that, the post-quantum ratchet (SPQR) keeps refreshing keys with ML-KEM-768. We use Signal's own open-source implementation unchanged. We don't write our own cryptography.

  • Symmetric ratchet: a fresh key for every message
  • Diffie-Hellman ratchet (X25519): heals after a key is exposed
  • Sparse post-quantum ratchet (ML-KEM-768): protects against future quantum computers

What our server sees

The relay carries envelopes it can't open

MEYA's relay only passes encrypted messages between phones and holds them while a phone is offline. Here's what that means in practice.

The relay can see

  • ·A random device ID for each phone
  • ·Which device sent an envelope to which device, and when
  • ·The size of each encrypted envelope

The relay never sees

  • ✓What your messages say
  • ✓Your private keys, which stay on your phone
  • ✓Your contact list or address book

We're working to reduce the routing information the relay sees. Envelopes are deleted from the relay once the recipient's phone confirms delivery.
